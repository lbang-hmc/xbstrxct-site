Aristotle's form of deductive argument: two premises sharing a middle term yield a conclusion. All *X*s are *Y*; *Z* is an *X*; therefore *Z* is *Y*. Its validity depends only on form, not on what *X*, *Y*, and *Z* mean. That is both its power and, as the penguin shows, its blind spot.
